Common questions

Why doesn't Biblia have NIV / ESV / NLT?

Those translations are copyrighted by publishers (Biblica, Crossway, Tyndale) who only license to registered commercial entities. As a single developer, I can't legally bundle them. The five translations Biblia ships with — KJV, WEB, BSB, ASV, and LEB — are either fully public domain or permissively licensed, which is why the app can be free and stay free.

Will you add cloud sync between my devices?

No. The whole point of Biblia is that nothing leaves your device. If you want cross-device sync, the giants (YouVersion, Bible Gateway) offer it — at the cost of an account, analytics, and a feed. Biblia is the alternative.

Will you add a "verses I read this week" social feed?

No. See above.

The audio voice sounds robotic. Can you add a real reader?

Biblia uses Apple's built-in text-to-speech because it works for every translation, plays in the background, supports CarPlay, and never requires a download. On iOS 18+, you can pick a Premium or Enhanced voice from Settings that's much more natural. A bundled human-narrated audio Bible would only cover KJV, would add about a gigabyte to the download, and would still need a managed download UX.

How do I report a typo or wrong verse number?

Email me with the reference (e.g. "John 3:16, ASV translation") and the issue. Scripture text is sourced from established public archives, so typos are rare, but if you find one I'll fix it in the next release.

How do I back up my highlights and journal?

Your data lives in the app's local database. Use a standard encrypted iCloud or iTunes device backup — your Biblia data is included. If you delete the app, the data is permanently gone (this is also a privacy feature). An export-to-Markdown feature is on the v1.4 roadmap.

Can I use Biblia on my Mac?

Biblia is built primarily for iPhone, with an Apple Watch companion. It will run on a Mac with Apple Silicon via "iPad & iPhone apps on Mac" — same code, same data. A dedicated Mac-optimized layout may come later.

Feature requests

Email them. I prioritize features that fit the "quiet Bible app" positioning: contemplative, offline-only, no account, no feed. Features that would require a backend, an account, or social mechanics are not going to ship — but I'm open to anything that makes the reader, study tools, or practice modes better.
